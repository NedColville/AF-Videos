Auroral Form Identification

Supplementary videos accompanying the scientific poster

About these videos

This page provides supplementary video examples of auroral forms identified in IMAGE Far Ultraviolet (FUV) imagery. The videos illustrate the morphology and temporal evolution of giant undulations (GUs) and omega bands, including examples recovered by our machine-learning model. They accompany Figures 1 and 6 of the poster and provide additional context for the events discussed therein.

Figure 1 – Auroral form examples

Figure 1a – Giant undulation

Example of a GU observed during a large geomagnetic storm, characterised by equatorward-pointing undulations in the dusk sector. The video illustrates the sunward propagation of the wave structure and its crests. This event was previously identified in a 2010 study by Henderson et al.

Figure 1b – Omega band

Example of an omega band, characterised by poleward-protruding structures in the dawn sector. As with the GU, the structure exhibits sunward propagation, corresponding to eastward motion in this sector. This event was identified by our model.

Figure 6 – Additional model detections

Figure 6a – Extended omega band train

An omega band event identified by our model. The event comprises an extended train of omega bands spanning more than 6 hours of magnetic local time (MLT).

Figure 6b – Structured omega band event

An omega band event identified by our model. This event exhibits interesting morphology, with tall, narrow, torch- or streamer-like structures, followed by rapid sunward flows.

Figure 6c – Previously reported giant undulation

A GU event previously reported in a 2007 study, which was recovered by our model despite not being included in the training data. Although its amplitude is smaller than that of the event shown in Figure 1a, its recovery provides an example of the model's ability to identify morphological features beyond those represented in its training examples. Sunward propagation is again apparent.

Figure 6d – Dawnside candidate event

A rare dawnside event, potentially exhibiting GU-like morphology, recovered by our model. Although the structure is less clearly defined than in the other examples, its occurrence at this MLT raises interesting questions concerning the possible drivers and spatial distribution of auroral forms.
